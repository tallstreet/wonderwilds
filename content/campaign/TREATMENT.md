# The Missing Moonlight — story treatment

Authoring draft, 14 November 2026. Narrative, educator and accessibility review are
pending. This treatment is not approved release content. `runtime.v1.json` now
defines finite actions and short dialogue for all 26 main missions, friendships,
four growth moments per friend, fossils, copied clues, the early island, sanctuary
and post-game boards. Welcome, Bridge and Bloom remain the first playtest checkpoint.
Runtime verification and audience review are distinct from authoring completeness.
The immutable v2 specification remains design history.

## The rules of the mystery

Moonlight travels through roots, waterways and eight beacon lines. During the Great
Storm, Vell fitted collectors with a mechanical instruction: fill the Observatory
reserve until its central release is opened. The warning network broke before that
release instruction could reach him. After the storm, Vell deliberately kept the
collectors running because a full reserve made him feel prepared. The machines
are not secretly malicious: their continued orders, and his refusal to investigate
the costs, cause the crisis. A label, a recovered warning and his signed ledger
let the player establish each part before confronting him.

Each local project bypasses a collector intake and restores that habitat's beacon.
It gives immediate useful light and stops further local damage. It cannot open the
sealed central store or return the moonlight already taken. The Observatory's
emergency return manifold needs eight grounded lines: bridge for support, garden
for living flow, tower for timing, mill for measured delivery, river for circulation,
refuge for overflow, lighthouse for direction and the Observatory for shared control.
With fewer lines, releasing the reserve would flood one route and darken another.
The final Accord beacon is the central line, restored during the confrontation.
Seven local seals permit entry; eight active lines permit the safe final release.

The lighthouse lens is a physical object, not a magic light reward. The player sees
its shipping cradle at the Meadow watchtower, tracks its removal in the mill ledger,
and recovers it from a reachable Cove maintenance store using a hand lamp and
Sail's marked ridge or tunnel route. Illuminate is earned **after** the recovered
lens is aligned. It is never needed to obtain itself.

Rowan travels with **Moss**, an individual mossback with a square satchel and a slow,
careful manner. Moss is not one of the twelve catalogue recruits or a taken starter.
Rowan's later second partner, **Reed**, is a long-legged marsh companion who enjoys
calling a rhythm. Neither changes the player's ownership catalogue. Rowan begins
by asking Moss to solve every problem alone, learns the cost at the river, and
ends by giving both partners chosen, complementary jobs. Moss voices his own needs.

## Meadow — noticing before chasing

**1. Welcome: The first expedition.** Hazel offers three free practices: Pip reflects
light onto a mark, Tansy makes room around a shoot and Nib steadies a stool. A short
supported packing invitation introduces help without testing eligibility. The
player chooses a travelling friend. During the Gathering preparations the bridge
beacon dims and a brass cart rattles away. Rowan wants to race after it; Moss asks
him to check the crossing. Hazel asks the player to notice what the cart left behind.

**2. Bridge discovery: The broken crossing.** Storm-split boards lie above cleanly
unscrewed moonstone mounts. The player records the difference and the brass curls.
Hazel changes her explanation: the storm did not take everything. The learning
invitation helps organise observations; a supported route preserves the exact clue.

**3. Bridge project: A safe way over.** Three marked fallen branches become three
planks through the woodcutter. Hazel lends braces before the player earns a kit.
No living tree must be damaged and no starter is required. Rowan suggests Moss
hold every brace; Moss chooses one. The laid planks visibly change the crossing.
The player can see a small island but cannot reach it before Current's ferry access.

**4. Bridge trial: One steady crossing.** The last traveller crosses while the team
manages gusts and braces. Hazel's three loan charges and universal moves work with
every starter. The Bridge seal opens the crossing, a second team slot and a permanent
brace kit. The small beacon relights, while a collector pipe still hums uphill.
The distinction between a local repair and the unreleased reserve is explained.
No second companion is granted here. Camp is now a reachable recovery destination.

**5. Bloom discovery: The garden that forgot morning.** Fern shows two equally
watered beds. Only the bed beside the collector has closed. Comparing them reveals
that adding water cannot replace the light being taken from roots. Tansy notices
the pulling sensation, Pip notices the pipe and Nib recognises its fastenings;
these are perspectives, never exclusive keys.

**6. Bloom project: Room to grow.** Two independent handles bring water to near and
far beds. The player chooses a winding wild border or ordered paths; both preserve
room for shoots and persist as the garden's appearance. Fern's free frames or a
companion shelter the seedlings. The invitation to check a plan is separate from
turn decisions. No answer failure damages plants or spends tactical resources.

**7. Bloom trial: Hold the morning.** The collector draught tests the prepared
shelters. Victory wakes the garden beacon and grants the seed kit and Bloom seal.
The uncovered label reads “Temporary Emergency Reserve. Continue until central
release.” Vell answers through its speaker: he will inspect the roots after the
reserve is full. Fern points out that the storm is over. Vell refuses to stop.
The first unchosen Pip or Tansy can now be helped and invited; Nib's invitation
remains after Echo. Declining leaves an invitation, not a lost friend. The player
may change their pair at camp, rest and return to brace the opening-bank alcove.
The island remains beyond the current. A two-friend practice is a game rehearsal,
not another source of milestone XP.

**8. Echo discovery: The message nobody heard.** Flutter taps a pattern beside the
hollow; people dismiss it as an echo. The player follows three visible vibration
marks and lets Flutter lead. They find the broken warning line and a shipping
cradle stamped LIGHTHOUSE LENS. Rowan says a louder call would have worked; Moss
points out that listening found it. Flutter wants a turn to conduct the repair.

**9. Echo project: Give the warning a voice.** The player reconnects three pictured
chimes using visible marks as well as sound. A free tuning fork covers every
required interaction. Flutter conducts, and the complete warning says the storm
has passed and the temporary collection should cease. A second note records the
lens transfer through the mill. Help never requires hearing a pitch or fluent reading.

**10. Echo trial: Keep the shutters open.** Changing gusts threaten to silence the
message. A rhythm friend can prepare another friend's progress; keeper equipment
supports a solo route. The Echo seal opens Orchard and permanent resonate access.
Flutter's invitation and the remaining starter invitations become available, with
Nib waiting at the picnic camp. Vell acknowledges receiving the restored message
but calls continued collection a precaution. The mystery has become a disagreement
about whose safety counts. Rowan races toward the river to prove he can help faster.

## Orchard — sharing the load

Harvest and Current begin after Echo and may be finished in either order. Each
keeper refers only to evidence the player actually has. Cove requires both seals.

**11. Harvest discovery: The locked harvest.** Cleo and Bramble have food waiting
outside a mill used as a reserve depot. Patter's ledger shows deliveries arrived
but were redirected uphill. Empty village stores and full sealed crates give the
player concrete evidence. If Current is already restored, Patter arrives by ferry;
otherwise the courier walks and explains the blocked landing.

**12. Harvest project: The missing gear.** The player chooses a marked ridge carry
or canal delivery to recover the sorter gear. Both provide the same gear and lasting
mill access; optional supplies affect convenience only. Bramble asks to carry every
crate. The player gives him a partner or uses the public trolley. Cleo assembles
the repair with the player rather than doing it alone. The signed instruction is
Vell's: keep packing after the storm until further notice.

**13. Harvest trial: A machine with the wrong job.** The sorter begins with jammed
braces and changes to growing packing vines. A team can switch its approach; a
single friend and loan tool can finish. Redirected crates reopen village deliveries,
while the sealed reserve remains intact. The mill becomes a camp crafting hub.
Cleo and Bramble offer friendships. The lens ledger identifies a Cove maintenance
store. If Current remains unfinished, the river pump is the next shared problem;
if complete, the keepers open the Cove road together.

**14. Current discovery: Leaves against the stream.** Floating leaves move uphill
because the main pump is drawing water into the reserve. Rowan has gone inside
with Moss. His dropped satchel and a failed signal show the player where to look.
Patter marks the safe bank and insists on a rescue plan before a contest.

**15. Current project: Let someone else hold it.** Moss holds a gate while Rowan
struggles with every valve. Moss is exhausted and asks for relief. The player uses
a free prop, a friend or Patter's rope to take the load, then opens the marked bypass
in a reversible visible sequence. Rowan must step back so Moss can rest. He admits
he kept saying “one more try” after Moss asked to stop. The player may respond
kindly or directly; both make his later acknowledgement specific, not punitive.

**16. Current trial: Give the river a way through.** The bypass must stay stable
while a controlled flow restores the landing. The Current seal grants ferry travel
and a free folding raft. Rowan apologises to Moss and asks Reed to join them for
a future shared expedition. Patter and Twizzle have accessible friendship projects.
Rowan offers a copied ridge clue in exchange for a copied tower clue; originals
remain in the guide. The opening island now holds the old network blueprint and
a keepsake. Its diagram foreshadows eight grounded lines without being required
to discover the same solution later at the lighthouse.

## Cove — following the light to its source

**17. Tide discovery: A glow near the missing lights.** Marina suspects the bright
visitor seen beside dark pools. The player compares soft paw marks with machine
wheel marks and follows a pipe, rather than blaming Glimmer from appearance. Luna
leaves a safe hand-lamp trail and Pebble checks tide markers with the player.
Glimmer is glimpsed carrying a stranded small creature toward higher ground.

**18. Tide project: Make room for a refuge.** A marked overflow route lets water
return without washing nests away. The player shelters displaced creatures and
finds collector valves under the same brass covers as the garden. Glimmer's trail
leads away from the intake and toward shelter. Marina changes her claim aloud:
“We saw Glimmer here. We did not see Glimmer take the light.”

**19. Tide trial: Hold the safe pool.** The encounter shifts from moving water to
bracing a refuge wall. Restored pools establish the Tide line and expose a dry
maintenance path to the lighthouse store. Glimmer's rescue marks clear the visitor;
the hidden tide garden is a sanctuary destination, not yet an automatic recruit.
Luna and Pebble can be invited through their own help projects. The collector's
central gauge rises despite local bypasses: stored light is now pushing against
the Observatory shutters. Vell closes the ascent instead of releasing it.

**20. Beacon discovery: The missing lens.** Sail matches the Meadow cradle mark to
the mill shipping record and the maintenance-store door. If the player missed the
optional island blueprint, the lighthouse wall contains the same network diagram.
A hand lamp illuminates ordinary labels before Illuminate is earned. Sail explains
why safe release needs all eight lines, including the central Accord line.

**21. Beacon project: Carry the lens home.** Choose the marked ridge or the accessible
maintenance tunnel. Sail lends a trolley and lamp; no illuminate ability, scarce
supply or particular companion is required. The physical lens is recovered once,
carried to the lighthouse and fitted with shared help. Rowan arrives with Moss and
Reed. Reed calls a walking rhythm while Moss supports the trolley at rests; Rowan
asks both whether they want to continue. Their changed plan is visible in action.

**22. Beacon trial: A direction for the light.** The recovered lens must hold its
alignment against shutters. Only after victory does the Beacon seal grant Illuminate
and a free lantern. The lighthouse points the restored lines toward the Observatory
and reveals the old maintenance ascent. Sail offers to travel, giving himself a
job rather than always cheering from shore. Luna can guide with the player or the
public lantern route. Rowan offers to keep the return trail open.

**23. Accord discovery: Enough is never full.** At the Observatory Vell shows the
reserve gauge. It rises, but so does pressure on the shutters. The player lays out
mounts, root comparison, the stop warning, signed ledger and rescue evidence.
Optional island and exchange clues add detail; mandatory evidence suffices. Vell
says he remembers holding a useless lamp during the Great Storm. The player can
name the harm, invite him to look, or ask who the reserve serves. All responses
retain accountability: the current damage cannot wait for his fear to disappear.

**24. Accord project: Eight paths, one release.** Vell initially locks the main
control. The old maintenance diagram permits a safe manual return, not a destructive
attack. The player reconnects seven local lines while Rowan, Moss and Reed keep
the exit marked and braced. Each region's earlier repair appears on the panel.
The eighth, central line requires Vell to stop filling and accept shared control.
He sees Fern's live garden signal flicker and hands over the crank. The player and
Vell open Accord together; a mechanical interlock confirms all eight grounded lines.

**25. Accord trial: Let the light go.** Three phases stabilise the intake (craft),
time the shutters (rhythm) and direct the return (gleam). Energy and tool charges
carry between phases. Every starter and the free loan equipment retain a solution.
Eight distinct beacon signals join the return; the reserve empties safely through
the network instead of one overloaded pipe. Vell owns his decision: “I kept taking
because I was afraid to have too little. You showed me who was going without.”
He agrees to dismantle intake orders with the keepers, rather than being instantly
forgiven. His collectors become public wayposts and monitored weather stations.

**26. Constellation ending: A gathering with room.** Back at the first bridge,
restored paths bring the keepers and friends from all three regions. The player's
garden design, remembered responses, team and discovered island appear in short
optional callbacks. Vell arrives with a machine that shares light and asks where
it will help; Fern asks him to start at the roots. Rowan proposes an expedition
where each partner chooses a role. Above the bridge the eight beams outline a
constellation pointing toward Glimmer's sanctuary. The finale completes the main
story. Sanctuary is a finite authored help quest that recruits Glimmer, with no
random chance, paid requirement, academic threshold or another player required.

## Personal endings and continued discovery

All twelve companions retain four distinct once-only learning-linked growth
milestones: first help, field partnership, personal project and Wayfinder expedition.
Their distinct short dialogue, recipient and story gates, keepsake rewards and
form flags are defined in runtime content; private tasks are selected through the
existing learning frontier. Their conflicts guide the scenes: Pip admits uncertainty;
Tansy permits wild growth; Nib shares a plan; Flutter leads listeners; Cleo accepts
an imperfect joint build; Bramble asks for support; Patter leaves time to rest;
Twizzle finishes a useful tune; Luna guides despite fear; Pebble redraws old marks;
Sail accepts an expedition role; Glimmer helps restore the account of the rescue.
Late recruits can complete unlocked milestones in order and reach the same form.

Post-game visits keep the changed world and offer finite habitat caches, personal
quests, Glimmer's sanctuary and authored trial variants. Replays never repeat
milestone XP or quest supplies. The island, fossils and NPC copies add discoveries
without hiding a required story key behind an optional trade. Human paired play
remains a separate implementation milestone with a solo route for every reward.
